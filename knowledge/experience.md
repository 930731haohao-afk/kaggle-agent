## Techniques by metric

### MAE / integer targets
- When the target is integer-valued, rounding continuous predictions to integers directly lowers MAE, and the CV gain carries over to the LB. | Evidence: s3e16, exp #1, LGB unrounded OOF MAE 1.35651 → rounded 1.33885; blend rounded 1.33812, Kaggle Private LB 1.34075 (gap only ~0.006).
- When the target's value range is a discrete set of combinations (not an integer lattice), snapping to the nearest training-set observed value is a small but consistent win over doing nothing / clipping only. | Evidence: s3e14, exp #2/#3, blend 341.02061 → snap 340.95856; exp #3 blend 340.75961 → snap 340.71180 (won in both iterations).

### QWK / ordinal targets
- For ordinal targets, regression + OptimizedRounder (tuning cutpoints on OOF QWK) far outperforms naive rounding — the single highest-leverage change for small-data ordinal problems. | Evidence: s3e5, exp #2→#3, same blend, naive round QWK 0.47191 → optimized cutpoints 0.52687 (+0.055, ~+10% relative).
- Under severe class imbalance (69.9x), naive rounding is even worse than a baseline with no feature engineering: rare-class predictions get pulled toward the majority mass and the symmetric-rounding assumption collapses. | Evidence: s3e5, exp #1 baseline 0.47871 vs exp #2 naive round 0.47191.
- For ordinal problems pick a regression head, not a multiclass head: QWK penalizes squared distance, and regression's continuous output can borrow signal from intermediate classes to place the extremely rare extreme classes (12–39 examples). | Evidence: s3e5, STATUS.md modeling decision; the final 0.52687 was achieved by the regression head; re-validated in the Phase B iteration: multiclass (class_weight=balanced) + expected-value decoding added to the blend as a diversity member scored only 0.52040 single-model post-rounder QWK (well below the tuned regression members 0.56244/0.56466), the weight search zeroed it, no contribution to the blend. | Evidence: s3e5, exp #11 (abandoned), 0.56769 = 0.56769 (no change).
- **For small-data (~2k rows) ordinal + QWK, the highest leverage is not adding features or models, but setting the Optuna objective directly to the post-processed (OptimizedRounder) QWK, run on the full 5-fold CV (the data is small enough that no fold-0 proxy is needed), rather than the traditional "tune RMSE first, then apply the rounder" — the most direct way to sidestep the discretized-metric trap, with gains far beyond cutpoint optimization or weight search alone.** | Evidence: s3e5, exp #4→#5, LGB tuned this way (TPE, 40 trials, 600s timeout, actual only 26s) then added to the pool, 0.52986→0.56293 (+0.03307); exp #5→#8, same method tuning CatBoost then adding it, 0.56293→0.56769 (+0.00476); cumulative gain over the previous best 0.52687 (cutpoints optimized but untuned) is +0.04082 (+7.7% relative).
- Nested (leave-fold-out) cutpoint fitting can gauge the size of the "cutpoints overfit the full OOF" risk: the risk is not fixed — it shrinks as the ensemble pool matures. | Evidence: s3e5, exp #7 (5-way blend, one tuned model dominating) full OOF 0.56293 vs nested 0.54649, gap +0.01644; exp #10 (6-way blend, two tuned models) full OOF 0.56769 vs nested 0.56393, gap shrinks to +0.00377.
- Risk (original record, partially mitigated by the nested diagnostic above): fitting cutpoints directly on the full OOF vector (not nested CV) carries mild OOF-overfitting risk; extreme classes may go entirely unpredicted (the s3e5 test set had no quality 3/8 predictions, and the new best after the Phase B iteration no longer predicts even quality 4, leaving only 5/6/7). | Evidence: s3e5, STATUS.md explicit risk note.

### SMAPE / time series
- When test is a future period (zero date overlap with train), you must validate with TimeSeriesSplit (split on unique dates); random KFold is an "interpolation-style" optimistic estimate that severely overstates. | Evidence: s3e19, exp #2 (TimeSeriesSplit) SMAPE 10.175 vs exp #3 (same features, same model, random KFold) 4.281 — a 2.4x difference purely from the CV scheme.
- Model ranking changes under extrapolation: with interpolation the three models are nearly equal (blend 0.4/0.2/0.4); under extrapolation LGB clearly leads and XGB is zeroed by the weight search — the actual submission should use the weights obtained under a CV that matches the test regime. | Evidence: s3e19, exp #2 weights LGB0.9/XGB0.0/CAT0.1 vs exp #3 weights 0.4/0.2/0.4.
- Right-skewed target (skew 1.75): train on log1p, invert with expm1, making the distribution near-symmetric (-0.22). | Evidence: s3e19, exp #2 uses log1p, together with the best blend above.
- **Counterexample (defining the boundary condition of the s3e20 "structure beats GBDT" pattern): proportional decomposition (total(date) × country_share trend extrapolation × fixed store/product shares) loses to GBDT on panel time series whose total series is itself hard to extrapolate; the weight search zeroed it**. Even though additive OLS verified that log1p(target) using only the three categorical columns (no dates at all) explains R²=0.974 of the variance — the structure genuinely exists — the decomposition model's solo OOF SMAPE 14.75 is still far worse than LGB 10.18. Oracle diagnostics (feeding true daily totals to isolate the share-model error) show the share-decomposition part at SMAPE 10.0–10.8, on par with GBDT rather than better: GBDT's native categorical splits already absorb the share structure, and the true bottleneck is "one-year extrapolation of the total" (a 5-year COVID-disturbed series: 4.45M→4.72M→4.52M→4.09M→4.88M, only 4-5 annual points with no reliable trend), a bottleneck that hits every method equally. **The durable dichotomy from contrasting with s3e20: structural signal beats GBDT only when the target is near-constant across years (s3e20 cross-year std median ≈3.1 → historical mean wins outright); when the total level itself drifts year over year and cannot be extrapolated (s3e19), structural decomposition adds no information GBDT lacks.** Side finding: extrapolating a drifting country share with a linear trend is worse than simply using the pooled mean (oracle SMAPE 10.84 vs 10.04) — the annual points are too few and too noisy; trend fitting is a net liability. | Evidence: s3e19, exp #4, blend 10.17540→10.17489 (gain from a finer weight grid, not RD; RD weight 0.0), abandoned.
- **Under TimeSeriesSplit, the Optuna fold proxy should be the "last fold", not fold-0**: time-series folds are not interchangeable — the last fold has the largest training window and a validation slot closest to the true test extrapolation regime. Targeting fold-5 SMAPE (40 trials in only 42s) tuned an LGB that is shallower and simpler (num_leaves 63→20, min_child_samples 20→38), again confirming that extrapolation regimes reward regularization; the tuned model's full 5-fold solo OOF is 10.14833 (beating every seed-bag member), and after entering the 6-way weight search via the "add to pool, don't replace" recipe it took 0.5 weight, blend 10.15721→10.01946, the largest single-round gain of that competition. **Honesty note: fold-5 is both the tuning target and 1/5 of the OOF, so 10.019 has a partly optimistic component (the tuned fold-5 score of 8.13 is selected); but the directional gain is real (folds 2-3, untouched by tuning, also slightly beat the original LGB). The more rigorous approach is to run the weight search on folds 1-4 only.** | Evidence: s3e19, exp #7, 10.15721→10.01946 (-0.137).
- The seed-bagging recipe also works in the SMAPE / time-series extrapolation regime, positive in both rounds (consistent with s3e9/s3e14): +LGB seed2024 gives 10.17489→10.16605; +LGB seed7 and +CAT seed2024 then give 10.16605→10.15721. | Evidence: s3e19, exp #5/#6.
- **Positive confirmation of the other pole of the "structure beats GBDT" dichotomy (the control group complementing the s3e19 counterexample): on panel time series where shares are constant across years, structural decomposition (Ridge on log1p = multiplicative decomposition) crushes GBDT**. On TPS jan-2022 (3 countries × 2 stores × 3 products; store ratio 1.742 unchanged year over year, product shares unchanged year over year, dow effect identical across all dimensions), structural Ridge solo fold-2018 SMAPE 4.19 vs GDP-normalized LGB 5.80 vs raw LGB 8.43; the blend weight search puts nearly everything on Ridge (0.95). Operationalizing the criterion: first check the cross-year variance of the shares — if near zero, go all-in on the structural model. | Evidence: tpsjan22, exp #4/#12/#13.
- **External macro data fixes the level extrapolation: country×year total ∝ GDP per capita (World Bank NY.GDP.PCAP.CD, free API, 2019 values fetchable on the spot)**. Diagnostic: if total(country,year)/gdp_pc is nearly equal across countries with only a common year drift remaining, then log_gdp in the design matrix + linear year_c for the common drift solves the "one-year total extrapolation" bottleneck (original claim: s3e19's common bottleneck disappears here because GDP is knowable — **this transfer claim was partially falsified by a real LB test on 2026-07-30**: on s3e19, a budget-matched single-config comparison shows wiring in GDP is worth only **−6.0 SMAPE** (no external 54.506 → feature-level 48.497, paired test significant), while the gap to the leader 4.67 is ~44 points. A global-multiplier probe further decomposes the residual: ×1.6 gives 32.93, ×2.2 gives 46.78, so ~15.5 points are **annual total level** error and ~33 points are shape error. That level value is unlearnable in-sample (the proportional relation only implies 1.05×; the auto_scale grid found 1.0). Conclusion: GDP shrank the bottleneck but **did not eliminate** it, consistent with line 27 of this file, "the true bottleneck is one-year total extrapolation, hitting every method equally" — line 27's diagnosis is the correct one). The GBDT-side counterpart recipe: change the target to log(num_sold/gdp_pc), -2.6 SMAPE (8.43→5.80); ablating year_c hurts badly (2017 fold 4.83→6.02). | Evidence: tpsjan22, exp #2/#3, STATUS.md.
- **Holiday features need "per-name × wide-offset windows", not a single pooled flag: the +3..+7 days after a holiday show a sustained lift (post-Easter +34% miss); per-holiday-name dummies over an offset window of -5..+10 are the single biggest lever (5.23→4.19); pooled flag→per-name alone already earned 5.46→5.23**. Tooling: the python holidays package (FI/NO/SE includes movable feasts such as Easter/Midsummer); pooling names across countries increases samples per coefficient. | Evidence: tpsjan22, exp #10→#11→#12.
- A fixed SMAPE shrink factor (global multiplier c<1) is directionally inconsistent across folds — annual level bias dominates, far outweighing the SMAPE-asymmetry benefit; do not use. Rounding to int for integer targets has a tiny stable gain (4.1855→4.1793). | Evidence: tpsjan22, train_v2 shrink sweep (2018 optimum c=0.99, 2017 optimum c>1.02), exp #13.
- **The aug-2022 "fitted-weight blend optimism" lesson holds again on SMAPE panel time series, and on a structure-dominated comp an entire tree search can yield zero real gain**: the 2026-07-28 tpsjan22 full-pipeline rerun (60-node harness_v3 tree search, root = structural Ridge 4.415985 pooled OOF), 9 structural-mutation lineages (alpha / Fourier order / holiday window / per-product / GDP offset / dow×product / recency weight) with none beating the root (best solo child 4.4172); the tree-search "champion" 37-member mega-blend OOF 4.40502 refits to an honest score of 4.4374 under leave-year-out weight refitting (optimism +0.032), and every fitted blend loses to root solo. Operational conclusions: (1) when a structural model's knobs were already dialed in during the linear stage, the tree search's marginal value concentrates in "using the honesty gate to veto blends" rather than finding new gains; (2) the honesty gate itself is high-variance with only 2 validation years (fit17→18 4.23 vs fit18→17 4.65); conservatively choosing solo is the right call; (3) the genuinely diverse member (LGB, 2 SMAPE points worse) gets honest weight ≈0 — weight search over a pool of homogeneous Ridge variants is pure noise fitting. | Evidence: tpsjan22 2026-07-28 rerun, experiments_tree_v3.json, STATUS.md honesty audit table.

### ROC-AUC (ranking metric)
- AUC is a ranking metric: adding scale_pos_weight/class_weights for imbalanced binary classification mainly perturbs the loss surface rather than improving ranking — a bad trade on small samples; removing the imbalance weighting + strengthening regularization is the fix. | Evidence: s3e3, exp #2 (48 features + imbalance weighting) 0.81901 → exp #3 (same features, weighting removed, LGB num_leaves 15→7, L1 0.5→1.0, L2 1.0→2.0) 0.83292 (+0.017).
- **The s3e5 recipe "set the Optuna objective directly to the final metric on full CV" (previously validated only in the QWK / discretized post-processing setting) transfers to AUC and works just as well, and AUC is a continuous ranking metric with none of QWK's discretization traps**: on small data (1677 rows), 50 trials of full 5-fold CV took only 111.3 seconds, using OOF ROC-AUC (rather than some proxy loss) directly as the Optuna objective; the optimum found is even shallower than the existing "shallow, strongly regularized" exp #3 LGB (num_leaves 7→3) yet with lighter regularization (reg_lambda 2.0→0.0012, controlling overfit via min_child_samples=60). | Evidence: s3e3, exp #4, LGB single model 0.832925 → 0.837305 (+0.00438).
- After tuning, the "add to pool rather than replace + seed bagging" recipe also stacks small gains on AUC, though the magnitude decays across iterations (+0.0044→+0.0005), consistent with the s3e14/s3e11 observations. | Evidence: s3e3, exp #5, 5-way weight search blend 0.837305 → 0.837776, weight concentrated on LGB_orig(0.3)+LGB_tuned(0.7), seed-bag versions weight 0.
- **The conclusion "removing imbalance weighting helps AUC" had only been validated on small samples (1,677 rows); the first cross-season generalization test (s4e1, 165,034 rows, bank churn prediction) ran the first bit-for-bit comparable ablation at large scale, and the conclusion holds in the same direction**: same 28-feature set, same 5-fold StratifiedKFold (seed=42), LGB with `is_unbalance=True` OOF AUC 0.893235 vs without 0.893650, delta −0.000415 — same direction as s3e3 but much smaller magnitude (at large sample sizes weighting perturbs the ordering far less anyway). Side finding: the 2026-02 legacy baseline of that competition (is_unbalance=True, CV 0.89653) looks far better than this unweighted version mainly not because of is_unbalance itself, but because the old surname target encoding computed its OOF with an independent 5-fold split (seed=99) "different from" the model's training folds — since the encoding groups (surnames) are shared between the two fold partitions, feature values in a model training fold could partially carry target information from that fold's validation samples; a more suspect source of score inflation than is_unbalance. After switching to fold-safe encoding using exactly the model's training folds, the same-config solo LGB score drops to the 0.8937 range. **Lesson: target-encoding OOF folds must be identical to the model evaluation folds; an independent split with a different seed "seems to avoid leakage" but is in fact another leakage path.** | Evidence: s4e1, tier3 r1 (is_unbalance ablation), 0.893650→0.893235 (Δ−0.000415); cf. STATUS.md (Feb 2026) exp#2 legacy baseline 0.89653.
- **s6e2 (heart disease, 630k rows) confirms the "remove imbalance weighting" direction on large-sample AUC for the third time**: default vs `is_unbalance=True` same-fold comparison, weighted OOF AUC 0.954993 < unweighted 0.955009 (Δ−0.000016) — three competitions (s3e3 small-sample, s4e1 165k, s6e2 630k) consistently confirm this prior is robust across sample scales, with magnitude shrinking as the sample grows (weighting's perturbation of a ranking metric naturally weakens with n). | Evidence: s6e2, stage 3.1 is_unbalance ablation, 0.955009→0.954993.
- **When train/test groups are fully disjoint (aug-2022: train products A–E, test F–I), AUC is a pooled ranking across groups — two things hold simultaneously: (1) simple LR beats shallow LGBM (raw OOF 0.5906 vs 0.5842); the per-group noise GBDT learns cannot extrapolate to unseen groups; (2) "within-group rank normalization" post-processing is a stable +0.0007 (0.59055→0.59131), because pooled AUC gets eaten by between-group prediction-distribution shifts.** CV must use leave-one-group-out GroupKFold, and the post-processing is directly verifiable on OOF. | Evidence: tabular-playground-series-aug-2022, exp #3 vs #5; rank-pp ablation in the full sweep table (all 40 configs agree in direction).
- Same competition, side note: after per-group z-scaling of the features, log vs no-log on loading barely differs and raw is actually slightly better (0.59055 vs 0.59027) — within-group standardization already absorbs most of the effect of monotone transforms. Blending LR+LGB adds only +0.00002 (noise level), consistent with the "blend-layer micro-tuning is only noise-level on AUC" prior; abandoned. | Evidence: aug-2022, exp #6.
- **The optimism magnitude of fitted-weight blends on AUC was directly quantified for the first time, and it is enough to flip the ranking: the 38-member kitchen-sink mega-blend full OOF 0.591955 (first place in the whole tree search), after leave-fold-out weight refitting 0.590944 — optimism +0.001011, and the honest score is below the root LR single model 0.591248**. When members are near-homogeneous (same LR family ±1 feature / different C) and the true gain space is <0.001, the weight search fits OOF noise; the afsis lesson (optimism ~+0.016, RMSE scale) holds on the AUC ranking metric too, merely shrunk to +0.001. **Operational conclusion: blend decisions on AUC problems must use leave-fold-out weight refitting as the honesty gate; if the honest score loses to the best single model, submit the single model or an unfitted equal-weight small ensemble (equal weights carry no weight optimism; but the member set still must not be picked by score threshold — this competition's 22-member loose-rule equal-weight blend 0.590510 was dragged down by weak members; the final pick was a two-member equal-weight blend of root + the most stable variant, 0.591645).** | Evidence: tabular-playground-series-aug-2022 (2026-07-28 rerun), exp #4 (mega-blend LOFO) / #5 (loose family) / #6 (final); tree node #52.
- **LOFO-selection (for each held-out fold, pick the best candidate config on the other 4 folds and apply it to the held-out fold) can serve as the honesty gate at the "model / member-set selection" layer, at zero cost (entirely on cached OOF)**: among this competition's 5 candidates (root, root+17 equal, 4-member equal, 13-member equal, solo17), full-OOF gaps are ±0.0004; the LOFO-selection honest score 0.591204 ≈ root 0.591248 — verdict: all candidate gains lie within the noise band, which prevented submitting the tree-search top-3 (all in-sample blend products) as champion. | Evidence: tabular-playground-series-aug-2022 (2026-07-28 rerun), STATUS.md honesty audit section.

### RMSLE
- RMSLE = RMSE on the log1p target: even when the target is not skewed (skew 0.019), optimize the metric directly with a log1p+RMSE objective, invert predictions with expm1, and clip ≥ 0. | Evidence: s3e11, exp #2/#3 both use this; the methodology's own gain is small (exp #1→#2 only -0.00013) but it is the precondition for optimizing the correct objective.

### RMSE / extremely skewed targets
- Extremely right-skewed targets (skew 10.2) must be log1p'd; linear models (Ridge) can blow up catastrophically in this setting, GBDT is robust. | Evidence: s3e20, exp #1, Ridge RMSE 23,917.63 vs LGB 32.75 (mean baseline 155.54).
- **A target lying on a discrete grid ≠ predictions should be snapped back to the grid (harmful for RMSE)**: the s5e10 target accident_risk lies on a 0.01 grid (only 98 distinct values), tempting the s3e14 "snap to nearest training observation" post-processing; but the same-fold comparison shows snapped RMSE 0.056095 > clip-only 0.056027 — squared error wants the conditional expectation (a continuous mean), and forcing continuous predictions onto the grid only adds error. Exactly complementary to s3e16's rounding for MAE: **it is the metric (not the target's discrete appearance) that decides whether to apply discretization post-processing**. | Evidence: s5e10, stage-3 prior check B, snap 0.056095 vs clip 0.056027.

## By data type

### Small samples (<10k rows)
- On small samples CatBoost can be consistently worse than LGB/XGB and get zeroed by the weight search — do not assume a three-model blend always helps; check each model's OOF first. | Evidence: s3e3 (1,677 rows), exp #2/#3, CAT 0.77663/0.76268 far below LGB 0.81901/0.83292, blend weights LGB=1.0 both times.
- **The "CatBoost is weak on small data" diagnosis is now clarified: native categorical handling (passing raw strings via cat_features rather than label/freq pre-encoding) closes much of the gap but cannot reverse the ranking — "encoding choice" and "model/data-scale mismatch" are two independent problems stacked, and the latter is the main cause.** Retrying CatBoost on s3e3 (1,677 rows) with native cat_features (7 categorical columns kept as raw strings) + `allow_writing_files=False` + explicit thread_count, OOF recovers sharply from the label/freq-encoded 0.762684 to 0.814259 (+0.0516; best config was shallow strongly-regularized trees: depth 3, l2_leaf_reg 16, lr 0.05), proving part of the original deficit was indeed the encoding; but even with native handling it stays 2.3 points below LGB on the same data (0.837305), and after joining the 6-way blend pool the weight search still gives it 0 weight (same verdict as without native handling). **Conclusion: "CatBoost is structurally weak on very small tabular data" is the durable lesson; native categorical handling is a necessary but insufficient mitigation, and there is no need to retry this model on data of the same magnitude.** | Evidence: s3e3, exp #6 (CAT_native diagnostic, solo 0.814259), exp #7 (6-way blend incl. CAT_native, weight search still gives 0, blend score identical in prob-space to exp #5 without CAT_native, 0.837776).
- Small samples + highly correlated engineered features → prefer adding regularization over adding capacity. | Evidence: s3e3, exp #3 gained +0.017 by tightening LGB regularization; s3e9, exp #2 likewise (see below).
- Large per-fold score variance on small samples (AUC 0.79–0.89) is normal, not a bug; after a clear gain, quit while ahead and avoid over-searching in the noise. | Evidence: s3e3, STATUS.md explicit plateau stopping criterion, stopped after one reflexion round.

### Duplicate rows / label noise
- Fully duplicated features with different targets (~56% of rows) = a label-noise ceiling: it rewards regularization and punishes capacity; LGB/XGB at default hyperparameters overfit badly, while CatBoost's ordered boosting is naturally more resistant. | Evidence: s3e9, exp #1, at default params the weight search gives CAT 100% (12.54287); exp #2 regularized LGB (leaves15/depth5/L1=2/L2=4) takes LGB 13.21→12.11.
- Regularization and feature engineering only work "together" — with the same features but no regularization, LGB/XGB still overfit. | Evidence: s3e9, exp #2, blend 12.54287 → 12.07347 (-3.74%), STATUS.md states it is their joint effect.
- **Counterexample (do not retry): "duplicate-row group target smoothing" (fold-safely replacing training targets with the mean target of the identical-feature group within the training fold) is useless and harmful for GBDT + squared loss** — the minimizer of squared loss is already the target mean over identical input rows; GBDT does this implicitly. Explicit smoothing adds no information and instead (a) changes the groups' effective sample weights, (b) introduces estimation noise from fold-local means for groups split across folds. Two of the three models got worse (LGB 12.11061→12.12277, CAT 12.07459→12.08207; XGB up slightly 12.12086→12.11109 but not enough to save the blend). The "label-noise ceiling" diagnosis was correct, but the "remedy" is already built into the loss function. | Evidence: s3e9, exp #5, blend 12.07347→12.08122 (+0.00775), abandoned.
- **Under a label-noise ceiling, seed bagging is the only remaining reliable gain direction (it still works after both tuning and denoising fail)**: Optuna full 5-fold CV tuning of LGB (60 trials, 371.6s) gave a single model at 12.12074, actually worse than the hand-tuned 12.11061, and the weight search gave it 0 — yet the 2nd and 3rd members retrained with the same tuned hyperparameters under different seeds each took 12.7%/12.9% weight; then seed-bagging the highest-weight CatBoost (77% → split 47.8/26.5) delivered the round's largest gain. Two rounds of seed bagging produced all of Phase B's improvement (12.07347→12.07143→12.07003), while Round 1 (denoising) and Round 2 (the tuning itself) contributed 0. Mechanistic reading: near the noise ceiling, any hunt for "a better single model" just churns in noise; only averaging over independent randomness (variance reduction) remains a free lunch. Set beside the s3e5 counterexample "seed-bagging an Optuna model that directly optimized the final metric is useless": seed bagging's power depends on member variance still being high — GBDT variance on noisy data is high, hence it works. | Evidence: s3e9, exp #6→#7 (+seed2 tuned LGB) 12.07347→12.07143; exp #7→#8 (+CAT_seed2, +seed3) 12.07143→12.07003.

### p>>n spectral data (MIR/NIR chemometrics)
- **Kernelization (Kernel Ridge RBF) is a bigger lever than any preprocessing choice**: on the same SNV+SG-d1 spectra, the best linear Ridge variant is 0.48668; switching to KRR-RBF (closed form; one eigh on K makes the entire alpha grid free) drops to 0.46661 (honest inner-CV). The old conclusion "with p>>n use Ridge; GBDT/stacking overfit" still stands, but **"Ridge" should be read as "regularization + the kernel trick", not "linear model"**. | Evidence: afsis-soil-properties, exp #2 vs #5.
- **CV on spectral data must first find the hidden "multiple rows per site" groups**: afsis's 15 spatial covariates take only 580 distinct values across 1157 rows — 565 sites each contribute a Topsoil/Subsoil pair. Plain KFold splits a site's two rows to opposite sides and leaks location information; a "spatial-signature GroupKFold" is the honest choice. Generic recipe for finding the group key: round + tuple-dedupe counting on the non-spectral metadata columns. | Evidence: afsis-soil-properties, EDA (580 unique sites / 1157 rows).
- **Do not log/winsorize an extremely skewed target under an RMSE metric**: afsis target skew reaches 7.45 (P) / 4.71 (Ca), so log1p is intuitively tempting, but the same-fold comparison is worse across the board (krrlog 0.47216, krrwin (p99 cap) 0.46725 vs raw target 0.45244). RMSE wants the conditional expectation; squashing the tail systematically underestimates high-value rows. No conflict with the log1p advice in the "RMSLE / extremely skewed targets" section — **there the metric itself is on the log scale, here it is not; the metric decides whether to transform, not the target's appearance** (same logic as the s5e10 snap-to-grid counterexample). | Evidence: afsis-soil-properties, exp #7.
- Multi-target problems (MCRMSE = mean of per-column RMSE) should pick hyperparameters and blend weights **per target**: afsis's per-target best (gamma, alpha) differ hugely (P consistently picks the strongest regularization alpha=0.3, the other targets pick 0.003–0.03), and the best preprocessing variant differs too. A single global hyperparameter set gets dragged around by the hardest column. | Evidence: afsis-soil-properties, exp #9 per-target weight table.
- **The magnitude of "per-target weights vs one shared weight vector" has been directly quantified: same 16-member pool, same greedy search, per-target 0.433071 vs one shared weight vector 0.452840 (a 0.0198 gap, ~4.6%)**. That is larger than any member-level adjustment; doing only one global weight vector on a multi-target problem forces the hardest column's (P's) weight preferences onto the other four. | Evidence: afsis-soil-properties (2026-07-28 rerun), tree node #21 vs #22.

### All-categorical features (encoding-benchmark style data)
- **When the features are all categorical, sparse full OHE + LogisticRegression is the model class of choice and crushes GBDT**: on 23 all-categorical columns (incl. 5 high-cardinality hashed nom columns, max 11,981 levels, train/test categories almost fully shared), LR (C=0.1, 16,552-dim sparse OHE) OOF AUC 0.803103 vs LGB + fold-safe TE 0.797328 (tuned ceiling 0.798865) vs LGB native categorical 0.776214. Mechanism: per-level coefficients of high-cardinality OHE + L2 shrinkage are equivalent to a smoothed target encoding, without TE's within-fold estimation noise; GBDT tree splits can only cut coarsely on nominal columns with tens of thousands of levels. On 300k rows, lbfgs sparse LR takes only ~3s per fold — cheap enough to sweep the entire C grid. | Evidence: cat-in-the-dat, exp #1/#2/#3; tree-search LGBTE branch best 0.798865 (node #35) still 0.004+ behind LR.
- **Counterexample (do not retry): adding explicit pairwise interaction crosses to an already saturated sparse OHE-LR is harmful across the board**: all 136 crosses of the 17 low-cardinality columns (+5k dims) drop LR from 0.803103 to 0.796458 (C=0.1); sweeping C (0.05/0.13/0.15) recovers at best 0.796539; adding ord_5×12-column crosses is worse still (0.78584); the nearly unregularized C=1.0 + richest feature set is the worst LR of the whole run (0.770255). A different root cause from "tree models learn interactions themselves" but the same conclusion — sparse main-effect OHE is near saturation on this kind of data; interaction dimensions only add noise and eat the regularization budget. | Evidence: cat-in-the-dat, tree-search LRPAIRS branch (node #6/#48-51), EXPL_LRLOWREG (node #57/#63/#64).
- Blends of near-homogeneous members (an LR family differing only in C) + a weakly heterogeneous GBDT give only noise-level gains on AUC: 38-member kitchen-sink weight search 0.803272 vs best solo 0.803103 (+0.00017); after leave-fold-out weight refitting 0.803231 (weight overfitting only +0.000041). Consistent with "blend-layer micro-tuning is only noise-level on AUC" (s3e7) and extends it to the member layer: member diversity is the blend ceiling; a C-grid family provides no diversity. | Evidence: cat-in-the-dat, exp #5, tree node #55.

### Highly collinear features
- Near-perfectly collinear columns (the 6 TRange columns at r≥0.999) can be safely consolidated; combined with importance-probe pruning it is actually better. | Evidence: s3e14, exp #2 (27 features) blend 341.02 → exp #3 (pruned to 21 features) 340.76.
- Collinearity can be turned into a feature: the residual `weight_resid` of a known identity (Weight≈Shucked+Viscera+Shell, r=0.993) is an effective engineered feature. | Evidence: s3e16, exp #1, the 24-feature set with weight_resid gives rounded blend 1.33812 vs generic baseline 1.35441.

### Low-signal data (all raw features |r|≤0.11)
- When all raw features are weak, finding "group structure" for fold-safe target encoding is the biggest lever (store-profile group means with R²≈0.06 already beat any single raw feature). | Evidence: s3e11, exp #2 (methodology) 0.29710 → exp #3 (+store_te) 0.29614; the gain comes mainly from store_te, far exceeding the methodology change.

### Geographic coordinate data
- Raw latitude/longitude have weak linear correlation (r≈-0.12/-0.06) yet are the top-two GBDT importances — geographic signal is nonlinear; trees split it themselves, so just feed raw coordinates + distance features. | Evidence: s3e1, EDA + exp #2 (with dist_nearest_city, KMeans geo_cluster) 0.56166 → 0.55877.
- Given trees can already exploit raw coordinates, adding a coarse-grained spatial target encoding brings no gain. | Evidence: s3e1, exp #3, +geo_te LGB 0.56109 → 0.56102 (-0.00007, noise level, not adopted).
- **Geo target encoding being useless does not mean geo feature engineering is exhausted**: on the same dataset, non-target-encoding raw geometric features (KNN(k=10) mean neighbor distance over combined train+test coordinates, nearest distance to coastline anchor points) still bring real gains, clearly above the noise-level result of exp #3 — the key distinction is that "target-encoding one coarse grouping" and "feeding more diverse raw geometric measures" are two different levers: the former is redundant for trees that can already split coordinates on their own, the latter provides density/coastline information trees can hardly derive from Lat/Long splits alone. | Evidence: s3e1, exp #6, LGB single model 0.56109 → 0.560444 (-0.00065, judged a real gain, adopted); exp #7 after full-pool retraining blend 0.557859 → 0.557088.

### Spatiotemporal data with stable cross-year structure
- Validate the "historical mean" baseline first: if the target is near-constant across years per (location, week) (cross-year std median ≈3.1), a pure location-week historical mean can beat all GBDTs outright. | Evidence: s3e20, exp #4, the 4-way weight search gives TE-mean 100% and all three GBDTs 0; TE-mean 22.6488 vs best GBDT blend 28.3424. Understanding the data beats model complexity.
- **Once the historical mean wins, the next layer of leverage is "denoising the mean itself"; three moves stacked in order total −6.6% (22.6488→21.1487), with GBDT weights still all 0 every round** (s3e20 Phase B, LOYO CV same-fold comparable throughout):
  1. **Empirical-Bayes shrinkage (generic for count-sparse group means)**: when cell-mean observation counts are tiny (exactly n=2 per fold in the balanced panel), shrink toward the higher-count parent group mean (loc mean, ~106 obs), `α·cell + (1−α)·loc`, with the α grid sweeping out 0.935. | Evidence: s3e20, exp #5, 22.6488→22.4897 (−0.159).
  2. **Down-weight anomalous years (partial trust, never full exclusion)**: down-weighting 2020 COVID-year observations to W2020≈0.29 when computing historical means — the single largest gain of the round; the key is the U-shaped curve: W2020=1.0 (untouched) 22.49, optimum near 0.25 at 21.65, 0.0 (full exclusion) 22.24 is worse again — the anomalous year still carries partially valid signal. | Evidence: s3e20, exp #6, 22.4897→21.6317 (−0.858).
  3. **Adjacent-week (week±1) smoothing**: weighted average of the cell mean with the same location's adjacent-week means (WNB≈0.28); window ±1 is optimal, ±2/±3 both worse (21.23/21.51). **Adjacent-week smoothing also "absorbs" move 1: after joint re-tuning, α converges to 1.0 — the two moves attack the same noise source (small-sample variance of cell means), so stacking them is redundant; one done well suffices (adjacent-week smoothing is the stronger).** | Evidence: s3e20, exp #7, 21.6317→21.1487 (−0.483); exp #8 (4a) wide-window counterexample.
- **Residual diagnostics confirm the "structure saturation" boundary condition extends to the residual layer**: LGB trained on the (y − denoised TE) residual, added back at any ratio 1.0/0.5/0.25/0.1, is always worse (22.07/21.39/21.21/21.16, monotonically approaching TE-only) — the sensor features cannot explain even the residual. Merged with the s3e19 counterexample: when the target is near-constant across years, structure does not merely beat GBDT — it eats all the predictable signal; no need to try "structure + GBDT residual" hybrids on this data type again. | Evidence: s3e20, exp #8 (4b), 21.1487 → all ≥21.1592, abandoned.

### Routine data-quality checks (validated across competitions)
- Common defects and treatments: delete constant columns outright (s3e3, three zero-variance columns); treat physically impossible values as missing and impute the median (s3e16, Height==0 in 24 rows); drop columns >90% missing + indicator flags for 10–90% missing (s3e20, dropped 7 columns, built 21 flags); recognize target top-coding (s3e1, MedHouseVal 4.92% capped at 5.00001, capping the RMSE ceiling). | Evidence: EDA sections of each competition's STATUS.md.

## CV design

- **Never compare scores across CV schemes; every experiment must be logged together with its CV scheme.** An apparent regression after switching schemes may purely mean the new scheme is more honest. | Evidence: s3e19, exp #2 (10.18) seemingly loses to baseline (5.32); diagnostic exp #3 proves the same config under the old scheme is actually 4.28 (better).
- Test is a pure future period → TimeSeriesSplit (split on unique dates); and make the actual submission with the weights from that scheme. | Evidence: s3e19, exp #2.
- Multi-year spatiotemporal data → Leave-One-Year-Out CV, which honestly evaluates cross-year extrapolation and supports leak-free target encoding. | Evidence: s3e20, exp #3/#4.
- Binned stratification for regression targets: small-sample noisy regression uses target-decile StratifiedKFold (s3e9, qcut 10); integer long-tail targets use binning + tail merging (s3e16, Age≥20 merged into one bin, fold MAE stable at 1.34–1.37). | Evidence: the STATUS.md of each competition.
- Severely imbalanced ordinal/classification targets → StratifiedKFold directly on the label, avoiding folds with zero extreme-class rows. | Evidence: s3e5 (69.9x imbalance), 5-fold Stratified on quality, per-fold std 0.033.
- No time/group structure, continuous target, train/test distributions nearly identical → plain shuffled KFold is the right answer; do not over-engineer. | Evidence: s3e1, s3e11, s3e14 (s3e14 per-column train/test mean differences <1%).
- Measured CV↔LB gap record: s3e16 OOF 1.33812 → Public 1.34356 / Private 1.34075, gap ~0.006 (CV mildly optimistic, trustworthy). | Evidence: s3e16, exp #1 Kaggle submission.
- **OOF gains found by tree search can genuinely transfer to the real LB, not just artifacts of internal OOF comparison**: s3e16 tree-search v2 (node #15) pushed the rounded MAE on OOF from 1.33812 to 1.33563, at the time only a CV-only inference; after rebuilding the test predictions and actually submitting, the Private LB improved from 1.34075 to 1.33859 (Public 1.34356→1.34315) — both boards improved, the first external (real-leaderboard) validation this tree-search recipe has received. Meanwhile the CV↔LB gap stayed the same order of magnitude and direction across the two independent submissions (about 0.003–0.0075, LB slightly worse than OOF) — not only is the gain itself credible, the gap's stability also grows more trustworthy as submissions accumulate. | Evidence: s3e16, exp #5 (`experiments.json` leaderboard field), Public 1.34315/Private 1.33859, submitted 2026-07-06, vs exp #1's Public 1.34356/Private 1.34075.
- Fixed seed + fixed folds enable cross-experiment comparison; a rerun after revert should reproduce byte-identically. | Evidence: s3e9, exp #4 reproduced exp #2's 12.07347 to the digit.
- **If a member's hyperparameters are selected on the full OOF, that member's OOF score carries selection bias; but the bias mostly washes out after blending, so "use nested scores for method selection, fit final weights on the full OOF" is a worthwhile compromise**. Measured on afsis: the same KRR variant scores 0.45244 when (gamma, alpha) are picked on full OOF, 0.46661 with inner-CV (picked using outer-train rows only) — single-model bias +0.014; but after swapping the whole pool to honest versions, the blend's nested score only moves from 0.44861 to 0.44817 (within noise). Lesson: **single-model leaderboards must use honest scores, or you will pick the wrong model; final reported numbers should also be nested; but there is no need to rebuild the whole pool for the blend's score.** | Evidence: afsis-soil-properties, exp #5/#6/#9.
- **If a tree-search driver reseeds only part of the linear stage's members into the tree, the blend branch's ceiling is structurally capped — the second form of "node-space structural missing parts", and an order of magnitude more important than the weight-search method**. The same afsis driver was run twice: run 1 reseeded only 4 of the 16 linear-pool members, so the kitchen-sink blend could only mix the 27 members the search itself produced, scoring 0.449717 — **losing to the linear blend 0.433219 it was supposed to surpass**; run 2 changed exactly one thing (reseeding the entire linear pool as first-generation branches via zero-compute cached-OOF reuse), same node kinds, same budget, and the champion became 0.428772. By contrast, the weight-search method's full range at 64 members is only 0.428772 (greedy) / 0.428899 (dirichlet) / 0.430371 (bagged greedy), and at 16 members the three differ by within 0.000006. **Lesson: a blend node's score is determined mainly by member-pool diversity, not by the weight-search algorithm; the driver's first duty is to feed every existing member into the tree (cached-OOF reuse makes this nearly free), not to tweak the weight search.** Filed alongside s3e9-v1's "node space missing ensemble nodes" recorded in 07_tree_search.md §1 as the same failure class. | Evidence: afsis-soil-properties (2026-07-28 rerun), exp #6 (run 1 negative result) vs exp #5.
- Cautionary counterpoint: the previous entry's first-instinct hypothesis ("Dirichlet(1,…,1) sampling concentrates toward uniform as member count grows, diluting the blend") **does not hold under measurement** — at 64 members dirichlet loses to greedy by only 0.000127. After proposing a mechanistic hypothesis, always measure it on the same pool; never write the hypothesis down as a conclusion. | Evidence: afsis-soil-properties (2026-07-28 rerun), tree node #73/#74/#75.
- **Greedy weight search (forward selection with replacement) itself has an optimism magnitude of about +0.016~0.018** (the 2026-07-28 rerun landed in the interval again: in-sample 0.428772 → leave-fold-out refit 0.444076, optimism +0.015304, 64 members), and it barely changes as members grow from 16 to 52 — the optimism comes from "weights fitted on the same OOF", not from member count. Quantify it via leave-fold-out weight refitting. Bagged greedy (Caruana-style member sampling) does reduce optimism (+0.0146 vs +0.0186) but its nested score is not necessarily better; try both and let nested decide. | Evidence: afsis-soil-properties, exp #4/#6/#9.

## Hyperparameter tuning (Optuna)

- Tuning only the strongest single model's hyperparameters is the high-ROI lever for the blend: LGB 50 trials (TPE) yields +0.0004 single model / +0.0005 blend; the optimum is "shallow, strongly regularized" (depth 3, lr 0.068, reg_alpha 2.14), again confirming that small/medium data rewards regularization over capacity. | Evidence: s3e7, exp #3→#4, LGB 0.898824→0.899215, blend 0.899395→0.899891.
- Tuning-budget control: full 5-fold per trial blows the time budget (50 trials × 5 folds ≈ 25 minutes; one timeout wipes everything); switch to a "single-fold (fold-0) proxy objective" for tuning and rerun the full 5-fold validation only for the winning config — 50 trials in just 300s, and the proxy ranking transferred (fold-0's best config was also best on the full OOF). | Evidence: s3e7, exp #4, the first full 5-fold attempt timed out at 25 minutes with no result; the fold-0 proxy finished in 300.2s and delivered the gain above.
- **Repeating the same tuning recipe on a second base model hurts the blend**: after tuning XGB the same way, its single-model score edges up (0.898765→0.898860) but the blend regresses (0.899891→0.899722) — both models converge to similar shallow-tree optima and ensemble diversity drops. Single-model score and blend contribution are not the same thing; after tuning the strongest model, the other base models' "suboptimal but heterogeneous" parameters are an asset, not a liability. | Evidence: s3e7, exp #5 (abandoned).
- **The tuned single model should be "added" to the pool, not "replace" the original member**: the Optuna-tuned LGB improved from 342.02154 to 341.68775 as a single model, but directly replacing the original LGB regresses the blend (340.75961→340.95316; diversity lost, weight forced onto XGB/CAT); a 4-way blend that "keeps the original LGB and adds the tuned version as an extra member" instead drops to 340.62702. This generalizes s3e7's "don't replace diverse members" lesson to the strongest model itself. | Evidence: s3e14, exp #4 (replace) 340.95316 vs exp #5 (append) 340.62702.
- **Seed bagging (same hyperparameters, different random_state) is the cheapest residual gain after tuning**: adding one more seed=2024 original-parameter LGB as the 5th member takes the blend 340.62702→340.59891; the cost is one 5-fold training (~52s). | Evidence: s3e14, exp #7, 5-way blend weights 0.2/0.15/0.2/0.25/0.2.
- **Counterexample: seed bagging is not a universal gain and can be completely useless for models tuned by Optuna directly on the final metric** — applying the same move once each to the Optuna-tuned (directly optimizing post-rounder QWK) LGB and CatBoost with seed=2024 bagging: both weight searches gave the new member 0 weight, blend score unchanged or slightly down. Likely reason: Optuna directly optimizing the final metric has already converged to a "shallow and stable" solution (same shape as the "shallow, strongly regularized" solutions found by s3e7's fold-0 proxy method), model variance is already low, so a second seed offers limited diversity. | Evidence: s3e5, exp #6 (LGB_tuned seed-bag) 0.56293→0.56293 (no change); exp #9 (CAT_tuned seed-bag) 0.56769→0.56716 (regression, not adopted).
- **On very small data (~2k rows), Optuna with full 5-fold CV is itself cheap; no fold-0 proxy needed**: opposite to the s3e7/s3e11 setting where a proxy objective is needed to control the time budget, 40 trials of complete 5-fold CV (each trial retrains all 5 folds) take only 26–46 seconds, optimizing the objective directly on the "post-processed final metric" rather than tuning an RMSE proxy first and then applying post-processing. This mode applies only when data size and model training time are both small enough; at larger scale or slower training, stick to the established fold-0 proxy recipe. | Evidence: s3e5, exp #5 (LGB full CV 40 trials, 26s), exp #8 (CAT full CV 40 trials, 46s).
- **Three competitions (s3e7/s3e14/s3e1) consistently validate the same "Optuna fold-proxy tuning → add to pool (no replacement) → seed bagging" flow**, even with completely different RMSE scales, data sizes, and feature sets: s3e1 fold-0 proxy 50 trials took only 74.3s (vs full 5-fold timeout risk); the tuned LGB added as the 4th member of a 4-way blend takes the blend 0.558768→0.557977, then adding a seed=2024 version as the 5th member gives 0.557977→0.557859 — the flow itself can be treated as the default opener for this class of "mid-size tabular regression / GBDT blend" scenarios, with no need to re-argue it each time. | Evidence: s3e1, exp #4/#5.
- **A 4th competition validates that the recipe transfers to "CatBoost as strongest member" with data scaled up to 360k rows**: fold-0 proxy 40 trials in 319s tuning CatBoost (depth/lr/l2/min_data_in_leaf/random_strength); the tuned version added to the pool takes the blend 0.296143→0.295781 (the round's largest single item); seed-bagging a 2nd and 3rd seed then gives 0.295781→0.295715→0.295648. Two new observations: (1) on large data the optimum is "deeper + higher lr" (depth 10, lr 0.082, min_data_in_leaf 33) rather than s3e7's small-data "shallow + strong regularization" — capacity/regularization needs invert with data size; (2) after tuning, early-stopping convergence takes ~300 iterations (original params ~1300), making single-fold training nearly twice as fast — tuning buys score and speed at once. | Evidence: s3e11, exp #4→#5→#6→#8.
- **After successful tuning, the weights may flow entirely to the tuned seed family**: the s3e11 weight search zeroed both LGB and original-parameter CAT (tuned CAT solo 0.29579 already beats the original 3-way blend 0.296143). No contradiction with s3e7's "heterogeneous members are assets" — keep the original members in the pool and let the weight search adjudicate (keeping them costs nothing; whether they win is decided by OOF). | Evidence: s3e11, exp #8 weights {LGB 0, CAT_orig 0, tuned×3 = 0.4/0.2/0.4}.
- **Deleting a member with weight 0 in two consecutive rounds costs nothing**: after dropping XGB, s3e11's 2-way blend scores exactly the same as the original 3-way (0.296143 = 0.296143), saving ~25s of training per round. Zero-weight verdicts can be executed with confidence; no need to keep members "just in case". | Evidence: s3e11, exp #3 vs #4.
- **⚠️ Boundary condition (first counterexample): when the target requires "rounding to integers", the recipe still holds on raw OOF but may fail to convert into actual gains on rounded OOF; decisions must be made on the rounded score, not the raw score**. s3e16 (with a real LB anchor, CV↔LB gap only 0.006, CV trustworthy) reran the same "fold-0 proxy Optuna-tuned LGB → add to pool → seed bagging" flow: raw OOF did improve monotonically (1.35589→1.35541→1.35533), but rounded OOF worsened in both rounds (1.33812→1.33850→1.33893). Reason: the tuned version changed only depth/lr/reg — objective, features, and CV folds all identical — so the new member is highly correlated with the original LGB in OOF space (insufficient diversity), and the small raw-score gain cannot cross the rounding's discrete boundary. Two consecutive rounds without rounded-score improvement → stop per protocol; no new version submitted. Read together with the "rounding lowers MAE" principle in the "MAE / integer targets" section: rounding itself is a stable source of gain, but **model selection / tuning decisions after rounding must be re-validated on rounded scores** — the "lower raw OOF wins" shortcut validated on RMSE-scale competitions cannot be carried over. | Evidence: s3e16, exp #3 (raw 1.35541 rounded 1.33850, worse than 1.33812), exp #4 (raw 1.35533 rounded 1.33893, worse still).
- Blend-layer micro-tuning (0.05→0.01 weight grid, rank-average) gives only noise-level differences on AUC (+0.000002 / −0.000007); not a reliable gain source. | Evidence: s3e7, exp #6, prob 0.01grid 0.899893 vs 0.05grid 0.899891 vs rank 0.899884.

## Ensemble / post-processing

- OOF weight search routinely eliminates weak models outright (weight=0) — this is a feature, not a bug; respect it: s3e3 CAT=0 (twice), s3e11 XGB=0 (twice), s3e19 XGB=0 under extrapolation (twice), s3e20 all three GBDTs=0. | Evidence: blend_weights in each competition's experiments.json.
- With three models, a plain simplex grid weight search is hard to beat with learned stacking: Ridge (non-negative) stacking is clearly worse. | Evidence: s3e14, exp #3, Ridge stack 344.04 vs simplex blend 340.76.
- An unoptimized 50/50 equal-weight ensemble can be worse than the best single model — weights must be searched. | Evidence: s3e20, exp #2, LGB 32.75 + XGB 34.44 equal-weight = 33.21 > LGB solo.
- Blends only reliably beat all single models when the models are of comparable strength. | Evidence: s3e1, exp #2, blend 0.55877 < LGB 0.56109/XGB 0.56297/CAT 0.56188.
- **Counterexample (bounding the previous entry): a weak single model does not mean it should stay out of the pool — weak but heterogeneous members can still earn substantial weight**. afsis's svr_raw solo MCRMSE 0.684 (best member 0.452, a 50% gap), yet the weight search still gives it 0.20 weight on the Ca target; svr_sg1_C100 solo 0.5215 takes 0.15 weight on the P target. Reconciled with s3e3's "weak CAT gets zeroed": **the pool admission criterion should be "are its errors uncorrelated with existing members", not the solo score; leaving weak members in the pool for the weight search to adjudicate costs nothing.** | Evidence: afsis-soil-properties, exp #4/#9 weight tables.
- All three post-processing staples are evidence-backed: integer rounding (s3e16, 1.3565→1.3389), OptimizedRounder (s3e5, +0.055), snap-to-grid (s3e14, ~+0.05~0.06); plus clip to the training target range as a safety net (s3e1, s3e11). | Evidence: see the entries in the "by metric" section.

## Feature engineering patterns

- **Missingness itself can be signal, and model-based imputation beats statistical imputation**: aug-2022's m3_na (fail rate 0.160 when missing vs 0.213) / m5_na (0.254) indicator features made the final best LR feature set; measurement_17 (second-strongest feature, 8.6% missing) was imputed with a "per-group HuberRegressor regressing on its 4 most-correlated measurement columns" — the imputation model uses only feature columns (no target), and with disjoint train/test groups, fitting per test group leaks nothing. | Evidence: tabular-playground-series-aug-2022, exp #5 final feature set [loading, measurement_17, m3_na, m5_na, measurement_2], OOF 0.59131.
- Fold-safe (K-fold out-of-fold) target encoding is the largest single gain source on group-structured data; unseen combinations fall back to the global mean. | Evidence: s3e11, exp #3 store_te (~111 combinations, 19 unseen combinations in the test set) contributed the main gain 0.29710→0.29614; s3e20, te_locweek (computed from other years, leak-free) reaches 22.65 by itself.
- Ratio / physical-domain features beat raw columns: s3e9 water_binder_ratio (corrected to total binder, Pearson -0.227 vs the traditional water-cement ratio -0.151), log1p(AgeInDays) (0.558 vs raw 0.334); s3e16 part-weight ratios, density, shell-to-meat ratio; s3e5 alcohol_x_sulphates (|Spearman| 0.550 vs single features 0.504/0.457; 7 of the top-10 importances are engineered features); s3e1 rooms_per_person (r=0.452, strongest new feature). | Evidence: the exp scores of each competition listed in earlier sections.
- **Too many features regress the score → pruning is a standard weapon**: s3e7 exp #2 (+14 features) 0.89788 is below baseline 0.89882; after pruning noise features exp #3 0.89939 overtakes; s3e14 pruning 6 features 341.02→340.76; s3e9 adding 3 interaction terms 12.07347→12.09483 then reverted. | Evidence: each competition's experiments.json.
- Tree models learn multiplicative interactions themselves: explicit product interaction columns (even with domain rationale) only add collinearity and dimensionality on small noisy data, no new information. | Evidence: s3e9, exp #3, +binder×log_age etc. 3 terms 12.07347→12.09483, reverted.
- **[Cross-season correction, important] The previous entry's prior "trees learn interactions themselves; explicit interaction columns are useless" is not universal**: running the same-fold on/off comparison of this prior, it was **confirmed** on s5e10 (12 base features 0.056061 beat 27 features 0.056069) and s6e2 (base13 beat 33 features), but **overturned** on s6e1 (22-feature set R2 beat 11 base, Δ+0.000094) — there the interaction columns genuinely carried new signal. **Lesson: this prior must be checked per competition with a same-fold on/off comparison before adoption; it cannot be applied as a universal rule. The same prior can receive opposite verdicts across competitions — the cleanest instance of "move priors together with their applicability conditions, and test before use".** | Evidence: s5e10 stage-3 check A, s6e2 same check, s6e1 counterexample (Δ+0.000094).
- Cyclical (sin/cos) encoding depends on context: effective when the target is genuinely calendar-driven (s3e19 time series, the 20-feature set was the final best); pure noise when the underlying date columns are near-zero correlated with the target (r 0.003/0.008) — deleting them improves. | Evidence: s3e19 exp #2 vs s3e7 exp #2→#3.
- Iteration stopping criterion: stop when gains diminish after 2–3 rounds (s3e7 two rounds, s3e14 two rounds, s3e3 one reflexion round), saving the budget for the next competition. | Evidence: each competition's STATUS.md states its stopping reason.

## Negative lessons (tried and failed)

- Adding class-imbalance weighting (scale_pos_weight/class_weights) for AUC targets → drags down every model; XGB/CAT even fall below their own no-feature baselines. | Evidence: s3e3, exp #2, XGB 0.80511→0.79196, CAT 0.80874→0.77663; exp #3 recovers after removal.
- Adding coarse geo target encoding when trees already use raw lat/long → noise-level gain, not adopted. | Evidence: s3e1, exp #3, -0.00007.
- After group target encoding (store_te) exists, adding same-group "non-target feature means" (per-combo mean of sales_ratio/weight_per_case/gross_weight) → all members and the blend regress across the board (0.295715→0.296200), reverted. The group key's target signal is already exhausted by TE; same-key feature aggregates are just redundancy + noise for trees; isomorphic to s3e1's "coarse group aggregates are redundant for trees that can already split on their own". | Evidence: s3e11, exp #7 (abandoned).
- Engineering: one long process running multiple iteration rounds — CatBoost, running in a sandboxed background, stalled 33 minutes with no output apparently because its default file logging (catboost_info) was blocked from writing; the whole process was killed by timeout and all completed training was lost. ## Ideas pending validation (EDA-backed but untested; do not treat as validated)

- (Validated on 2026-07-04 and moved to the "SMAPE / time series" section: the s3e19 proportional decomposition has been tested, verdict counterexample — the share structure is natively absorbed by GBDT and total extrapolation is the common bottleneck; do not retry on the grounds of "stable shares" alone.)

### balanced accuracy (imbalanced multiclass)
- **balanced accuracy INVERTS the AUC imbalance-weighting rule: here class balancing is MANDATORY.** balanced accuracy averages per-class recall, so the majority class is capped at weight 1/K; class_weight='balanced' (LGB) / auto_class_weights='Balanced' (CAT) / sample_weight (XGB) is essential (predict-all-majority scores only 1/K). Do NOT transfer the "remove imbalance weighting helps AUC" prior to this metric. | Evidence: s6e7 (health_condition, 86/8/6% split), LGB/XGB/CAT all class-balanced -> CV balacc 0.949; blend 0.94994 -> Public LB 0.94939.
- **Per-class posterior multiplier tuned on OOF beats plain argmax for balanced accuracy.** After balanced training, grid-search a minority-class probability scale (e.g. x1.5) on OOF balanced accuracy; shifts predictions toward minorities (pred dist 0.81/0.07/0.12 vs true prior 0.86/0.06/0.08). | Evidence: s6e7, blend raw->adjusted lifted balacc, adj=[1,1.5,1.5].
- **CatBoost cat_features reject NaN** — fill categorical NaN with a "missing" string first (LGB/XGB handle NaN natively; only CatBoost errors). | Evidence: s6e7 CatBoostError object_idx NaN -> fixed with fillna("missing").

### grid/cellular-automaton inverse problems (per-cell MAE)
- **When the forward rule can be verified exactly, synthetic data = an infinite training set**: first verify the forward simulation bit-for-bit in EDA (dead boundary, 2000/2000 sampled reproduction), then use a generator "density U(0.01,0.99) → 5-step warmup → start → delta steps → stop, discard empty boards" aligned to the train marginals (density percentiles agree to 4 decimal places); this allows training on 10-12 × ten thousand synthetic boards per fold, with OOF computed on the real train only. | Evidence: conway-s-reverse-game-of-life, cnn_v1/v2.
- **A single delta-conditioned CNN beats separate per-delta models** (0.11575 vs 0.11848 search-grade): making delta a one-hot input channel shares all convolution weights, more data-efficient. A residual CNN (ch96, depth8, lr3e-3, D4 symmetry augmentation + 8x TTA) is a strong architecture for 20x20-grid inverse problems; depth12 is consistently worse under a 10-epoch budget. | Evidence: conway experiments_tree_v3.json; final OOF MAE 0.110352 vs all-zero 0.14513, per-cell LGB (7x7 window) 0.12252.
- **The optimum for MAE (per-cell error rate) is the per-cell marginal probability, not a single self-consistent preimage**: forward-consistency greedy flipping (forward-mismatch 0.099→0.068) actually worsens the MAE of every delta (0.1127→0.1165) — reverse GoL is many-to-one; pursuing evolvable consistency sacrifices marginal calibration. Per-delta threshold fine-tuning (d1:0.51, d2-5:0.49) gives a small stable gain (0.11039→0.110352). | Evidence: conway exp #3 (NEGATIVE), exp #8.
