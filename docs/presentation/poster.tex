% ---- 1 · The question a competition can answer -----------------------------
\begin{secblock}
\blocktitle{1\sep The question a competition can answer}
\claimpar{A competition scores, from outside, the three things a human
  investigator normally supplies --- \kb{judgment} about the problem, an
  \kb{error model} for the improvement claimed, and the \kb{honesty} of the
  claim itself --- so an agent's design decision here can be measured rather
  than argued.}
The scale is unforgiving: the tightest top-two private gap in the table in block 3
is \kb{0.00003}, and rerunning an agent unchanged moves its score by a
comparable amount --- which is what makes both the judgment and its measurement
hard.
\pgap
Every experiment is logged, so every number here comes from a logged run. One question dominated the build:
\vspace{4.5mm}

{\subheadsize\bfseries\color{accent}%
  Where should external knowledge enter the pipeline?\par}
\end{secblock}

% ---- 2 · The design decision -----------------------------------------------
\begin{secblock}
\blocktitle{2\sep The design decision: knowledge enters upstream}
\claimpar{The architecture has \kb{one} design decision --- where external
  knowledge enters --- and both placements were measured: downstream
  injection, among the models being blended, moved scores by about
  $10^{-5}$ in our own implementation, so knowledge now enters \kb{before any
  modelling}, at problem identification.}
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.915\linewidth]{fig1_arch.pdf}}
\figcap{The LLM reasons and decides at every stage. \kb{Purple} marks the
  injection layer: the problem dossier, the external-data module it feeds, and
  the typed operators it emits into the search. The leaderboard sits outside
  the agent.}
\vspace{5mm}
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.97\linewidth]{fig3_treeex.pdf}}
\figcap{One search tree. Every node is a complete, executable solution scored
  by cross-validation; a lineage is abandoned once its children stop improving;
  a \kb{blend node over cached out-of-fold predictions} competes with single
  models and here becomes the global best that is submitted. 37--224 evaluated
  nodes per lane.}
\vspace{5mm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item In a blind sweep of the benchmark the layer flagged \kb{exactly the
    competitions that need external data} and stayed silent on every other one.
  \item \kb{Every} controlled external-data arm beat its matched baseline on
    the real leaderboard, each one paired-significant.
  \item \kb{No local signal we tested selects the \emph{form} of the
    injection.} The one surviving selector was killed by its first
    pre-registered prediction, so the layer races both forms inside the search.
\end{itemize}
\end{secblock}

% ---- 3 · Local validation cannot arbitrate ---------------------------------
\begin{secblock}
\blocktitle{3\sep Local validation cannot arbitrate}
\claimpar{A claim about placement cannot be checked with the agent's own
  numbers: between agents no validation score settles anything --- one lane's
  pooled out-of-fold SMAPE of \kb{6.706} sat against a real leaderboard score
  of \kb{49.40878}. Every verdict is a real submission, judged by an error
  model taken from Kaggle's own two-way split of the test set.}
One submission gets a public and a private score; the shift between the halves
is shared across agents and cancels in the between-agent gap, so a verdict
needs the ordering to \kb{replicate on both halves} and the gap to \kb{exceed
its own movement}. my-agent's benchmark lanes held no Kaggle credentials, so none could
consult a leaderboard or submit; a separate operator step scored the frozen
files. my-agent alone carries a cross-competition \kb{experience
library}; no ablation of it was run, so the standing cannot be attributed to it.
\pgap
On that rule, 20 competitions $\times$ 3 pairings $=$ \kb{60 duels, all
adjudicated: 38 decided, 22 undecidable} --- not ties, but readings the test
refuses to make. \kb{my-agent takes 16--8 of its 24 decided duels
(66.7\%)}, against 11--15 of 26 (42.3\%) for each frozen yardstick; the
denominators differ because a duel is decided per pair.

% ---- 4 · Where it loses is where the design predicts -----------------------
\begin{secblock}
\blocktitle{4\sep Where it loses is where the design predicts}
\claimpar{The standing says who won; where it loses says why: my-agent's two
  worst losses fall on the only two competitions here with a \kb{mature,
  high-quality public solution} --- NVIDIA's kernel reproduction dominates there, search wins
  the rest --- and that mechanism, not the standing, is the result.}
On tps-sep-2022 it scores \kb{28.49605} SMAPE against 5.48351 for NVIDIA and
9.72095 for AIDE; its own public and private scores there (28.45070 /
28.49605) put the sampling noise two decimal places below the gap it loses by.
On tps-jan-2022: 6.29895 against 4.63651.
\pgap
The standing is ours to qualify too. Dropping s3e19 alone moves it from
66.7\% to \kb{72.7\%}, and one duel sits \kb{exactly} on its measured variance
floor: the rule requires the gap to \emph{exceed} the floor, compared in
\kb{exact decimal}, so it stays undecidable rather than a seventeenth win.

% ---- 5 · What transfers ----------------------------------------------------
\begin{secblock}
\blocktitle{5\sep What transfers}
\claimpar{Judgment, measurement and honesty were the three things under test;
  the placement result, the paired test and the audit answered them in that
  order. What transfers is not the ranking but the discipline that produced
  it: \kb{placement of knowledge is an engineering choice with a measurable
  answer}, and a ranking published without an error model is the author's own
  noise.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \kb{State the error model with every claim.} A third of our own duels
    cannot be called, and the winner moves with the composition of the
    evaluation set.
  \item \kb{Freeze the yardsticks.} Improving a yardstick mid-study makes
    earlier and later data incomparable, so neither was touched, even where we
    found weaknesses.
  \item \kb{Isolate mechanically, then read the transcripts --- including your
    auditor's.} Sandboxes stop the leaks you anticipated; reading what it
    actually did catches the ones you didn't.
  \item \kb{Placement, not amount.} The dossier decides \emph{whether} to
    inject; nothing local decides \emph{how}, so both forms race inside the
    search.
\end{itemize}
