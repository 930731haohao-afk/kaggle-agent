% ---- 1 · Motivation --------------------------------------------------------
\begin{secblock}
\blocktitle{1\sep Motivation}
\vspace{2mm}
\pbul\kb{Challenges of Kaggle competitions}\par\vspace{5mm}
\psitem{A competition requires the expert loop}{This includes validation design,
  feature extraction, model selection, and ensemble building. The top entries are separated by a very small margin.}
\psitem{Data preparation is repetitive}{The same cleaning, split
  design, and drift checks are done on every dataset.}
\vspace{2mm}
\pbul\kb{Why an AI agent}\par\vspace{5mm}
\psitem{A competition is a \emph{scorable} task}{Every competitor delivers a number, so it can be optimized automatically.}
\psitem{A competition requires tireless, systematic search}{Every experiment is recorded and should be reproducible.}
\psitem{Recent success of agents for Kaggle competitions}{Notable cases include tree search over code~\cite{aide2025}, and tree search fed injected research ideas~\cite{aygun2026}.}

% ---- 2 · Workflow ----------------------------------------------------------
\end{secblock}

% ---- 3 · Results — Kaggle benchmark ---------------------------------------
\begin{secblock}
\blocktitle{3\sep Results}
Our agent ``Sinica'' was run in audited sandboxes for all 23 competitions (20 ordinary competitions in Table 1 and 3 special competitions in Table 2). The results of the 20 ordinary competitions were scored on the real leaderboard, while the results of the 3 special competitions were scored offline with MLE-bench~\cite{mlebench}. We compared these results with the results delivered by two other agents, AIDE~\cite{aide2025} (tree search over code) and the NVIDIA Kaggle Agent~\cite{nvidia2026} (kernel reproduction). \kb{Among the 23 competitions, our agent ``Sinica'' obtains the best performance with 13 wins and 10 losses.}\par
\vspace{3mm}
\emph{Remark:} MLE-bench is an offline benchmark that replays finished Kaggle
competitions with their original data and metrics.\par

% ---- 4 · Disadvantages of the agent-based approach ------------------------
\begin{secblock}
	\blocktitle{4\sep Disadvantages of the agent-based approach}
	\vspace{2mm}
	\pritem{The agent is strong at coding, weak at judging}{The agent efficiently works through whatever problem it is given. However, it does not ask whether a change is worth making. This often leads to a poor design choice that yields bugs.}
	\pritem{The agent can only target the local validation score}{As a result, the local validation score can look outstanding while the real leaderboard score is mediocre.}
	\pritem{The agent often buries the main question in detail}{AI-written reports put too much focus on technical details, drifting away from what the reader wants to know.}
	\pritem{The agent has bad writing habits}{Redundant headings and reinvented terms often come back in every draft. Verifying and modifying them by hand is exhausting.}
	\end{secblock}

% ---- 5 · Practical advice for building AI agents --------------------------
\begin{secblock}
\blocktitle{5\sep Practical advice for building AI agents for data science}
\vspace{2mm}
\pritem{Build an isolated environment before running the agent}{This prevents any access to the leaderboard or another run's files.}
\pritem{Justify the design before building the pipeline}{This mitigates redesign cost, debugging time, and a full rerun of earlier competitions.}
\pritem{Inject external knowledge before data modeling}{This often improves the score. However, if those external data are injected at the final step, the score shows almost no improvement.}
\end{secblock}
