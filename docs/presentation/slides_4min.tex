% ============================================================= 1 MOTIVATION --
\begin{frame}{1 $\cdot$ Motivation}
  \vspace{1mm}
  {\small\kb{Why these tasks are hard}\par}
  \vspace{1.5mm}
  {\small
   $\cdot$ A competition demands the whole expert loop --- validation design,
   features, model selection, ensembling --- and the top entries separate at the
   \kb{fourth or fifth decimal}.\par\vspace{1.2mm}
   $\cdot$ At that scale \kb{local cross-validation is an unreliable compass}:
   a pipeline's own score routinely disagrees with the leaderboard.\par}
  \vspace{3mm}
  {\small\kb{Why an AI agent}\par}
  \vspace{1.5mm}
  {\small
   $\cdot$ Tireless, systematic search: \kb{37--224} candidate configurations
   per competition, every experiment logged.\par\vspace{1.2mm}
   $\cdot$ The same disciplined protocol on 20 competitions in a row --- a
   consistency a person cannot hold.\par}

  \note{Competitions demand the whole expert loop, and the top of the
    leaderboard separates at the fourth or fifth decimal, so tuning skill
    distinguishes nobody --- and at that scale a pipeline's own cross-validation
    routinely disagrees with the leaderboard. What an agent brings is tireless,
    logged search: between thirty-seven and two hundred and twenty-four
    candidate configurations per competition, and the same protocol run twenty
    times without drifting. (32s)}
\end{frame}

% ============================================================== 2 WORKFLOW ---
\begin{frame}{2 $\cdot$ Workflow}
  \vspace{0.5mm}
  \centerline{\includegraphics[height=0.66\textheight]{fig1_arch.pdf}}
  \vspace{1.5mm}
  {\scriptsize\color{mut}The LLM reasons and decides at every stage; the
   \kb{problem dossier} classifies the task and injects external data
   \emph{upstream}, before any modelling. Lanes hold no Kaggle credentials;
   submissions are scored post-run.\par}

  \note{The pipeline reads top to bottom. An LLM reasons and decides at every
    stage, gradient boosting does the fitting, and a tree search over complete
    candidate solutions is the optimisation loop. The one design decision that
    mattered is where outside knowledge enters. My first version put it
    downstream, at the blend --- measured, it was worth ten to the minus five.
    So it moved upstream, into a dossier stage that classifies the task before
    any modelling. The lanes hold no Kaggle credentials, so nothing inside a run
    can touch a leaderboard. (40s)}
\end{frame}

% =========================================== 4 ADVANTAGES & DIFFICULTIES -----
\begin{frame}{4 $\cdot$ Advantages \& Difficulties}
  \vspace{1mm}
  {\small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.30\textwidth}p{0.63\textwidth}@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Expected} & \textbf{Experienced}\\
  \midrule
  {\itshape\color{mut}The agent would quickly beat most humans.} &
    It leads the standing on all 20 scored lanes, but its wins
    concentrate where no mature public solution exists.\\
  {\itshape\color{mut}One run and one score decide a winner.} &
    Score noise leaves \kb{22 of 60 duels undecidable}; an explicit error model
    became mandatory.\\
  {\itshape\color{mut}A shared experience library only ever helps.} &
    One lane wrote its findings back two minutes before the next lane started;
    that lane read its neighbour's entries and was re-run clean.\\
  \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}\par}

  \note{Three things I expected, and what actually happened. I expected the
    agent to beat most humans quickly: it does lead the standing, but its wins
    concentrate where no mature public solution exists --- where one does, the
    reproduction agent beats it. I expected one run and one score to decide a
    winner: score noise leaves twenty-two of sixty duels uncallable, which is
    why the error model exists. And I expected a shared experience library to
    only ever help: one lane wrote its findings back two minutes before the
    next lane started, that lane read its neighbour's entries, and it had to be
    re-run clean. (43s)}
\end{frame}

% ============================================================ 5 DISCUSSION ---
\begin{frame}{5 $\cdot$ Discussion}
  \vspace{1mm}
  {\small
   $\cdot$ \kb{Put knowledge injection upstream.} At the blend stage it
   measured near-nothing and was abandoned; moved into problem identification
   it flags exactly the competitions that need external data.\par\vspace{2mm}
   $\cdot$ \kb{Local CV is the only feedback while you iterate.} A competition
   returns no score until submission, so an error in problem-level judgment
   leaves CV looking healthy while the leaderboard says otherwise.\par\vspace{2mm}
   $\cdot$ \kb{Version the agent, not just the code.} An architecture change
   invalidates every competition that ran before it --- start the benchmark
   over rather than mixing versions in one table.\par\vspace{2mm}
   $\cdot$ \kb{Isolate mechanically, then audit the transcript} --- and audit
   the auditor: ours exempted every command starting with \texttt{uv}, so the
   one command it existed to catch was never examined.\par}
  \vfill
  {\color{mut}\scriptsize github.com/930731haohao-afk/kaggle-agent\par}
  \vspace{1mm}

  \note{Four things I would tell anyone doing this. Put knowledge injection
    upstream --- at the blend stage it bought nothing. While you iterate,
    local cross-validation is the only feedback you get, and it can look
    healthy while the leaderboard says otherwise. Version the agent, not just
    the code: an architecture change invalidates every run before it. And
    isolate mechanically, then read the transcripts --- including your
    auditor's: mine was exempting the commands every lane actually issues, so
    the one command it existed to catch was never examined. Thank you. (30s)}
\end{frame}
