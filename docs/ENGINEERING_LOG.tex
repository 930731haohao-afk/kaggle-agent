\begin{center}\textbf{\large Why this document exists}\end{center}
\noindent The main report answers what we learned about agents. This one answers what went
wrong while building the machinery that produced those answers, and it is separate for a
reason: the two questions have different readers and different standards of interest. A
result belongs in the report if it changes what someone should believe about agent
benchmarking; a defect belongs here if someone rebuilding this system would hit it too.

Every defect below shares one shape, which is the only genuinely transferable thing in these
pages. \textbf{A produced artifact with no consumer is indistinguishable from a working one
--- in every log, every score table, and every coverage metric.} Nothing errors. The number
that should have moved simply does not, and no mechanism notices. Five of the nine defects
found on 2026-07-30 were of exactly this kind, and three of those were found only because
fixing the previous one prompted a check of the next layer down. That progression, more than
any individual bug, is the argument for tracing consumers rather than counting declarations.

The material here was originally interleaved with the results in the main report. It was
moved out because a reader assessing the benchmark's conclusions should not have to read an
engineering diary to reach them --- and because the diary is more useful when it is allowed
to be a diary, ordered by failure class rather than by narrative convenience.

\section{The Two Mid-Complexity Re-Runs, and the Voiding Rule}
\label{app:reruns}

Two of the nine mid-complexity cells were re-run, and the rule deciding which first attempts
were voided is asymmetric on purpose: \textbf{a failure caused by the harness is voided; a
failure caused by the method stands.} Voiding a method failure would quietly optimize the
yardstick; keeping a harness failure would score the environment instead of the agent.

\paragraph{my-agent/SIIM: killed by our own watchdog.} The lane first scored 0.92944 but was
killed at 150 of 480 minutes by the stall watchdog, whose 30-minute quiet threshold cannot
distinguish a vision lane between fine-tunes from a dead one --- a harness defect of exactly
the same family as Failure 3 below (health judged by the wrong signal). The threshold was
loosened to 90 minutes of quiet plus three consecutive confirmations, so a kill now requires
105+ minutes of confirmed silence, and the lane was re-run to completion: 394 minutes,
0.93529. The first attempt is archived.

\paragraph{Version identity across my-agent's cells (recorded, not disclosed in the main
report).} The three mid-complexity lanes did not all run the identical skill version: us-patent
and ventilator predate Stage 0.5; SIIM ran after and executed the dossier, which declined
external data, so the modeling code path is identical as far as tracing shows --- a traced
argument caught by file timestamps, not a rerun-and-confirm. The two firing-class lanes are a
further deliberate variant (full v5, pinned knowledge library). None of this touches any
per-competition three-way comparison; it bounds only the claim that my-agent's own methodology
was constant across the whole report.

\paragraph{my-agent/s5e1 (firing class): environment kill, relaunched.} The lane's first
attempt exited after 6 minutes with no submission (rc=1) when the session hit a usage-limit
reset --- an environment failure under the voiding rule. It was relaunched unmodified after
the reset and completed in 41 minutes. Recorded here because the rule requires every
first-attempt voiding to leave a trace, however boring.

\paragraph{NVIDIA/ventilator: a session accident, voided; the method finding, kept.} The full
reconstruction is in the next section. The 8.558 first attempt was voided because it measured
a session accident rather than the reproduction method; the re-run under an identical budget
scored 0.40093. What is \emph{not} voided is the finding the accident exposed: process
discipline is part of agent capability, and one-shot reproduction is all-or-nothing when it
lapses.

\section{Reconstruction of the Voided Ventilator Attempt}
\label{app:ventilator}

The driver log and the lane's own artifacts reconstruct the failure precisely --- and it is an \emph{execution-discipline} failure, not a time-budget one. The harness granted the full 4h budget; the session lasted \textbf{5 minutes} and exited cleanly. The frozen vote rule selected the highest-voted kernel (a literal \emph{starter} tutorial, \path{theoviel/deep-learning-starter-simple-lstm}, 1{,}166 votes), the agent ported it quickly, then launched the $200\times5$-epoch training \emph{in the background} --- violating its explicit foreground-only instruction --- logged ``training running\ldots\ waiting on monitor events,'' stopped calling tools, and thereby ended its own session; the orphaned training process died after \textbf{2 of 1{,}000} fold-epochs (37.6s of training; fold-0 validation MAE 1.28 $\to$ private 8.56), and the salvage submission shipped with no sanity gate. Under the fairness protocol the result stands: the environment delivered its budget, and the failure belongs to the frozen method's execution. The re-run, with the prompt hardened against the backgrounding pattern, used 109 minutes and scored 0.40093 --- so the method itself is competitive on this task, and the 8.56 measured only the lapse. The episode extends the ``self-assessment cannot veto output'' family (main report, Experiment 4) with a new member: \emph{process discipline} (foreground execution, session-lifetime awareness) is itself part of agent capability, and one-shot reproduction is all-or-nothing when it lapses, while iterative search always holds a submittable solution.

\section{Isolation: The Mechanisms, and Every Time One Failed}

The main report states four isolation conditions and what enforces each, because the three-way
comparison is meaningless without them. This section is the other half: how each is built, and
the record of every occasion one of them broke. That record is the more useful half --- each
failure was silent, and each was found by a different accident.

\subsection{Data isolation}

\path{build_isolated_roots.py} constructs one root per competition per side, linking only files
the Kaggle API's own manifest lists as official --- including members extracted from official
archives, which is the case a naive filename check gets wrong. Officialness is judged by the
manifest rather than by convention, because the failure it exists to prevent looked exactly like
a normal working directory.

\paragraph{Failure 1.} The harness copied my-agent's entire working directory to AIDE, exposing
processed features and tuned hyperparameters. Four AIDE results were voided and re-run.
Detection was not automatic: it came from inspecting which files the champion code actually
opened. Nothing in the logs distinguished a lane reading its own features from one reading
somebody else's.

\subsection{Resource isolation}

\path{lane_lock.sh} is a mutex built on atomic directory creation, holding the owner's PID so a
dead holder's lock is reclaimed rather than wedging the queue, with bounded waiting and a
timeout that logs contention explicitly rather than proceeding in silence.

\paragraph{Failure 2.} Before the mutex existed, the rule was written down but not enforced:
one lane ran at load average 17 while another had the machine to itself, on the same
competition. This is the defect that voided RUN1 and is the reason the mutex was written.

\paragraph{Failure 2b, found 2026-07-30.} The mutex was a no-op for the one driver that called
it from Python. It acquired inside a throwaway \texttt{bash -c} subshell, whose \texttt{EXIT}
trap fired \texttt{lane\_release} the moment the command finished --- and the ownership test
passed, because that subshell genuinely was the recorded owner. \path{lane_lock.log} shows
\texttt{ACQUIRED} and \texttt{RELEASED} stamped in the same second while the training it was
supposed to protect ran for another 95 minutes. No overlap resulted, purely because nothing else
tried to start. Fixed by holding the lock in the long-lived parent process, in an implementation
wire-compatible with the shell version so the two exclude each other rather than each honouring a
private lock --- verified by having a shell acquirer refused while Python holds it.

\subsection{Instruction isolation}

The rule is that a prompt may carry environment facts and metric thresholds only: no modeling
recipes, no other side's scores, no workaround discovered by another side. This one is enforced
by protocol and an incident log rather than by code, which is a real weakness and is disclosed as
one --- there is no mechanical check that a prompt is free of leaked strategy.

\paragraph{Failure 5.} The headless driver's prompt enumerated six pipeline stages but omitted
tree search, and the agent followed the prompt rather than the skill. Five competitions ran a
truncated method. The measured cost on afsis-soil-properties: the truncated run produced 0
tree-search nodes and CV MCRMSE 0.44817 in 32 minutes; the corrected run produced 80 nodes and
0.444076 in 17 minutes --- better \emph{and} faster. The fix was to make the prompt state that it
is not the pipeline definition: \emph{follow the skill as written}.

\paragraph{Failure 5b, found 2026-07-30.} A driver launched AIDE without pinning
\texttt{exec.timeout=1800}, so it silently fell back to its own 3600s default --- twice the
per-step execution budget every comparable run had used. Caught by a condition check in the
successor driver, which compares each run's recorded \texttt{timeout} and \texttt{steps} against
the pinned benchmark values before counting the run as conforming. The affected runs were
re-done. This is why AIDE lanes must be launched through the existing drivers rather than a
hand-assembled command line.

\subsection{Scoring integrity}

\paragraph{Failure 3.} The watchdog judged health by whether the machine was busy overall, so a
dead lane went unnoticed for a night while other work kept the load high. Fixed with per-driver
checks: no completion marker and no live process means dead, regardless of what else is running.

\paragraph{Failure 4.} Aggregation took the best score across all experiments, which counted
leakage experiments that had been deliberately tagged as diagnostic. Three competitions'
numbers were wrong. Found by scanning logs for the exclusion tags that were already there and
already being ignored.

\paragraph{On the shape of these.} Failures 1, 3 and 4 all had the same structure as the
injection defects in the previous section: the artifact existed, the log looked normal, and the
number was wrong for a reason no mechanism was watching for. In each case the evidence needed to
catch it was already being written down --- which files were opened, which processes were alive,
which experiments carried an exclusion tag --- and nothing was reading it.

\section{The Adversarial Execution-Path Sweep, 2026-08-03}

The 07-30 audit covered the v5 injection stack only. Before running any new competition,
the same treatment was applied to everything a fresh lane actually executes: search
machinery, arm construction, the external-data layer, three evaluators that had not been
touched in months, and the lane pipeline including the skill instruction files (the agent
executes those too, so an instruction bug is a bug). Five hunters, every finding then
handed to a separate verifier instructed to refute it and to attempt a repro before it
counted. \textbf{34 findings survived verification}; the 15 on a new lane's execution path
were fixed the same day (commit \texttt{a245b37}).

\paragraph{One defect was self-inflicted, and is the reason this sweep was worth running.}
The per-fold target-encoding work of 07-31 renamed a variable inside the evaluators' fold
loops (\texttt{Xtestdf} to \texttt{Xte\_fold}). \path{make_v5_arm.py} patches the generated
arm evaluator by matching that exact line to install the ratio inversion. The anchor stopped
matching. Nothing failed: the assertion counted the \emph{validation} inversions, found
them, and passed --- so the next ratio arm built would have trained on the ratio and never
inverted it, producing a confidently wrong submission. The fix discovers the operand instead
of assuming it, but the lesson is the older one: a regex contract between two files is a
consumer trace waiting to break, and only a test that builds an arm end-to-end would have
caught it.

\paragraph{The gates that recorded instead of gating.} The \texttt{ratio\_target} and
\texttt{log\_offset} preconditions --- proportionality, covariate volatility, nominal-series
detection --- each appended an ``unrealized: precondition failed'' note to the ledger while
the target transform was installed anyway and the operator was simultaneously booked
\emph{realized}. A ledger reader saw both entries and could believe either. Two of the
checks were also blind in the places that matter: \texttt{proportionality\_check} passed any
year whose covariate was missing, because \texttt{NaN > threshold} is \texttt{False}; and
\texttt{covariate\_volatility\_check} filtered out the fetched base year, so the transition
into the first panel year --- the one that year is fetched for --- was never measured. The
gates now refuse, with an explicit \texttt{force} override that is itself recorded.

\paragraph{The cost guard that cost more.} \texttt{eval\_blend\_with\_cost\_guard} ran the
full-budget weight search, measured the wall time, and \emph{then} re-ran at a coarser
budget and returned the coarser result --- paying more than the unguarded path and recording
a strictly-no-better blend. It now predicts from previous blend costs on the same tree and
decides before spending; a completed result is never discarded.

\paragraph{Silent over-exclusion in the self-exclusion filter.} The rule that stops a re-run
reading its own recorded answer matched competition slugs as substrings. \texttt{s3e1} is a
substring of \texttt{s3e19}, \texttt{s3e11}, \texttt{s3e14} and \texttt{s3e16}; \texttt{s5e1}
of \texttt{s5e10}. Those lanes silently discarded every sibling competition's evidence. The
error direction is conservative --- less knowledge, never leakage --- so no reported score is
affected, but it means the experience library was quieter in those lanes than the design
intended, and nothing said so.

\paragraph{Four more, briefly.} A v5 baseline arm reused the competition's own OOF cache
directory, whose keys are node ids, so its node~7 could read the pre-existing tree's node~7
vectors. The explore-burst patience counter counted function invocations rather than
evaluated nodes, so failed evaluations and deduplication burns ended bursts early. The v1
reopen-once fallback wiped every plateau flag including the burst-seed sanity gate's
verdicts. And the batch drivers for both reference methods never took the lane mutex --- they
only polled for each other --- so resource isolation had quietly reverted to a convention,
which is the defect that voided RUN1.

\paragraph{What the sweep says about the 07-30 finding.} Six of the fifteen fixed defects
are the same shape as that day's: something was produced, recorded, or asserted, and no
mechanism checked that anything consumed or honoured it. The count is not the point --- the
point is that the shape recurs in a codebase whose authors had just spent a day removing it,
which is the argument for making the trace mechanical rather than remembered.

\section{Generalizing the Source Layer, and the Gate That Failed Open}

The injection layer had a property worth keeping and a limitation worth removing, and they
were the same property. The dossier may name only whitelisted sources, so every joined column
has a stated origin, a stated leakage rule and a pinned vintage --- and every whitelisted
source was keyed by country and time, so the layer fired on exactly the country-panel
competitions and nothing else. The architecture generalized; the vocabulary did not.

Letting the agent fetch whatever it judges useful destroys the property that made the layer
worth having, and it does so in a way no test can detect. On Kaggle the most valuable
``external data'' for a competition is frequently a dataset another competitor assembled,
occasionally containing the test labels; a temporal as-of join with a correct lag will happily
join the answer, because nothing is dated wrong. The defense therefore cannot be a check. The
dataset whitelist was replaced with a \textbf{domain} whitelist --- the host must be a listed
general-purpose reference publisher, and data-sharing platforms are excluded outright --- plus
an admission procedure whose five gates (structural, leakage, snapshot determinism, coverage,
reachability) each refuse with a stated reason. Admission also requires a \emph{falsifiable
pre-registration}: a predicted magnitude and the comparison that would prove it wrong, on the
same discipline as \path{docs/preregistrations/}. The first source admitted through it is
\texttt{cpc:titles}, chosen precisely because it has no country, no date and no time axis at
all --- a shape the old layer could not express. Stage 0.5 may now propose a new source and
write its fetcher when nothing admitted fits, but it may not admit its own source.

\paragraph{The gate that failed open.} Four auditors then attacked the finished layer along
different lenses, every finding handed to a separate verifier required to reproduce it before
it counted: \textbf{15 survived, 13 were refuted}. The worst by a distance was in the rules
gate --- the module that decides whether a competition permits external data at all, and whose
false positive is a disqualification rather than a bad score. Its permit list contained the
bare substring \texttt{allowed to use external data}, which matches inside the single commonest
Kaggle prohibition, ``Participants are \textbf{not} allowed to use external data''. No forbid
pattern covered that wording. The subsystem's hard stop returned \texttt{permitted} and cited
the prohibiting clause as its evidence. Four related holes traveled with it: the forbid list
missed Kaggle's canonical ``you may not use data other than the Competition Data'' family,
bolded negation and table rows; the matcher took the first hit anywhere in the document, so one
permission-shaped sentence in an FAQ outvoted a binding prohibition; a clause permitting
\emph{pretrained models} was read as permitting joined data; and matching a lowercased copy
while slicing the original text let a \texttt{permitted} verdict be recorded with an empty
quote, violating the module's own stated invariant that a decision requires a quote. The
matcher is now clause-scoped and negation-aware, collects every hit rather than the first, and
treats a pretrained-only permission as a conflict to be read by a human.

\paragraph{The defect the gate above it was written to prevent.} \texttt{check\_coverage}'s
docstring names, as its reason for existing, countries with no calendar silently receiving
all-zero holiday flags. The runtime path was still doing exactly that: \texttt{flag\_feature}
discarded the merge report and never read the fetcher's \texttt{unmatched} list, so those rows
carried a systematically zero feature while the ledger booked the operator realized and left
\texttt{unrealized} empty. Underneath it, \path{merge_holiday_flags} normalized the
competition's dates but never the \emph{calendar's} --- a calendar whose dates were strings,
\texttt{datetime.date} objects or tz-aware Timestamps matched nothing at all, on any panel,
and returned an all-zero column with a clean report. A zero-flag total across a whole panel is
now a raised error, because it is a join failure and never a plausible calendar.

\paragraph{And a gate that could not refuse.} The leakage invariant built
\texttt{usable = [p for p in ps if p <= row\_period - lag]} and then asked whether any element
of \texttt{usable} exceeded \texttt{row\_period - lag} --- false by construction. The violation
count was always zero, so the gate reduced to ``a period column exists with at least two
values'', and a source declaring \texttt{lag=500} over a one-year span passed it. Of the
thirteen refuted findings, most were attacks on the domain gate (userinfo tricks, punycode,
open redirects, arbitrary module import through the fetch spec) that its design already
handles. The pattern in what \emph{survived} is the one this project keeps rediscovering, and
it is not an intruder: something was produced, recorded or asserted, and nothing checked that
it was consumed or honoured.

\section{The Nine Defects of 2026-07-30}

A single day's audit, recorded because the pattern matters more than the individual entries.
Six were surfaced by a deliberate adversarial audit of the v5 stack; three more were found
while verifying the fixes for the first six --- which is itself the finding.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{The experience library handed a rerun its own recorded answers.} The agent is
  instructed to consult \path{knowledge/experience.md} before EDA and before modeling, and
  retrieval matched on metric and task tags only. By July the library held the benchmark runs'
  own conclusions, so re-running a competition would have returned the feature set and
  ensemble choice that produced its recorded score. Fixed by dropping bullets whose evidence
  field names the competition being solved --- the citation was already in the text, which is
  why the fix was cheap. On aug-2022 it drops 4 of 9 matching bullets, including the line
  carrying that competition's own final score.

  \item \textbf{The stored prior mandated the operator form that loses.} \texttt{TASK-TS-FUTURE}
  required \texttt{ratio\_target} and forbade \texttt{join\_feature} alone, on the sound
  argument that a tree cannot extrapolate a feature past its training range. Two leaderboards
  say otherwise. The prior now requires racing both forms under a rule fixed in advance, and
  records the counter-evidence.

  \item \textbf{Three operators were emitted into a key nothing read.} \texttt{objective},
  \texttt{blend\_member} and \texttt{encoding} were written to \path{plan["node_configs"]} and
  booked ``realized''; a repository-wide search found the write, the docstring describing the
  contract, and no reader. Fixed with a translator that turns them into real seed nodes and
  writes back which node ids carried which operator.

  \item \textbf{LightGBM determinism was unpinned.} Scores are stored to six decimals and the
  champion is a bare \texttt{min}, so the default multi-threaded histogram build could flip
  selection on cross-process variance. Pinned across all ten evaluators.

  \item \textbf{External-data vintage was unpinned.} The cache keyed on indicator and year
  range but not vintage, so a refresh could substitute a revised series --- and the World Bank
  rebases constant-price GDP periodically, rewriting all of history. Now a tracked lockfile
  with an assertion.

  \item \textbf{\texttt{carry\_forward} filled backwards in time.} Unmatched rows were filled
  with the panel's single latest year regardless of direction, which is correct for a
  genuinely-future test row and a leak for an early training row. Now checked per row.

  \item \textbf{\texttt{postprocess} spoke a different vocabulary than its evaluator.} The
  dossier asked for \texttt{round\_to\_int} / \texttt{clip\_min} / \texttt{global\_scale};
  \texttt{maybe\_postprocess} recognized only \texttt{auto\_scale} / \texttt{scale}. Requests
  were silently dropped. Found while verifying the fix for defect 3.

  \item \textbf{The ledger claimed a verification that never ran.} \texttt{split\_policy} and
  \texttt{postprocess} were booked ``honored by the evaluator'' unconditionally --- the
  dispatcher runs on dataframes and cannot see the evaluator. A static checker now reports 4
  of 5 such requests implemented and 1 with no mechanism at all. Found while writing up
  defect 7.

  \item \textbf{A lazy-evaluation refactor silently disabled \texttt{ratio\_target}.} Making
  panel-key resolution lazy (the fix for a different problem) left one call referencing the
  removed eager variable, raising inside the operator's own try block. Invisible on arms built
  before the change; the first arm built after it would have shipped a ``ratio'' arm with no
  ratio in it. \textbf{The coverage ledger caught this one} --- it appeared as an explicit
  unrealized entry with the exception text rather than as a plausible-looking arm. An
  AST-based guard now asserts no bare panel-key name survives in that function.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What the sequence shows.} Defects 7, 8 and 9 were each found while verifying the
fix for the one before it. None would have been caught by running the system and looking at
scores, because in every case the scores were plausible. They were caught by asking, of each
declared capability, \emph{which line of code consumes this?} --- and following the answer
until it either reached an execution site or ran out. That question is cheap, mechanical, and
was not part of the process until this day.

\paragraph{One defect was caught by the architecture rather than by a person.} Defect 9
surfaced as a ledger entry because the coverage ledger was built, after defect 3, to record
unrealized operators with their reasons. That is the only instance here of the system
catching its own failure, and it is the argument for the ledger existing: the eight others
required someone to go looking.
